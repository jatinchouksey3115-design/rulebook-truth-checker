# Student Academic Handbook — Demo Corpus

> Synthetic corpus created for the Vibe Coding Round. It is not an official university regulation.

The handbook is deliberately written as a realistic institutional rulebook. Three internal contradictions are planted for evaluation; they are documented separately in `contradictions.md`.


## 1. General Academic Rules

### Rule 1
The University Academic Handbook sets the baseline rules for undergraduate and postgraduate study. It applies to students enrolled in regular programmes unless a programme-specific regulation expressly says otherwise. A programme-specific regulation is read together with this handbook; it does not automatically cancel a general rule. Students are responsible for checking the version of the regulations that was in force for their academic session. Departments should publish programme-specific notices in the same place as the handbook so that students can see the relationship between general and local requirements.


### Rule 2
The academic year is divided into teaching terms followed by an examination period. A student is expected to register for the subjects shown in the approved curriculum and to complete any prerequisites before taking a subject. Registration is not complete until the student has cleared any administrative hold that the registration office has identified. A student who changes programme, takes an approved semester break, or returns from an academic leave may have a different study sequence, but the normal rules continue to apply unless the approving authority records an exception.


### Rule 3
The handbook uses several terms consistently. “Student” means a person currently registered on an academic programme. “Teaching day” means a day on which scheduled instruction or an officially announced academic activity takes place. “Examination” includes a university or department examination that contributes to a course grade. “Continuous assessment” includes quizzes, assignments, laboratory work, presentations, and other assessed activities conducted during the teaching period. Where a rule uses a percentage, the percentage is calculated from the records maintained by the responsible academic office.


### Rule 4
Official notices may add operational details, such as a room change or the exact time at which a form closes. A notice should not be interpreted as silently changing a substantive academic requirement. If a notice appears to conflict with a regulation, the matter should be referred to the Academic Registrar or the authority named in the relevant section. Students should keep copies of notices that affect their registration, assessment, fees, accommodation, or eligibility.


### Rule 5
The university aims to apply rules consistently while allowing documented exceptions through the authorities named in the handbook. Informal permission from a class representative, laboratory assistant, or another student is not a substitute for an approval required by regulation. When an approval is granted, the student should retain the approval message or letter because later offices may need to verify it.


## 2. Attendance and Eligibility

### Rule 6
Attendance is recorded by the instructor or another person designated by the department. Students are expected to attend scheduled classes, laboratories, tutorials, seminars, and other compulsory academic activities. Unless a programme regulation states a higher requirement, a student must have attendance of at least 75 percent in a course to be eligible to sit the end-term examination for that course. Attendance is calculated from the sessions for which the student was expected to be present, not from the total number of sessions offered to students in every section.


### Rule 7
The attendance register should be maintained throughout the term. Students should report an apparent attendance error promptly to the department rather than waiting until examination registration. A correction normally requires evidence such as the original attendance record, an instructor confirmation, or a timetable record. The department may publish the attendance figure before the examination form closes so that students have an opportunity to raise a discrepancy.


### Rule 8
The ordinary attendance rule is intended to apply uniformly across courses. Missing a class for convenience, travel, a private event, or an unplanned personal engagement does not by itself create an automatic academic exemption. A student who is unable to attend because of a recognised circumstance must follow the separate procedure for that circumstance. The existence of a procedure does not mean that every request will be approved.


### Rule 9
For the ordinary case, the 75 percent threshold is the eligibility test. A student below the threshold is not normally permitted to sit the end-term examination. The department should communicate the status before the examination where practicable. If the student's records contain an approved exception, the exception must be considered before a final eligibility decision is recorded.


### Rule 10
Attendance rules do not change the grading weight of a course. A student who is eligible to sit an examination is still subject to the assessment scheme published for that course. Conversely, completing assignments or laboratory work does not automatically remove an attendance deficiency. Attendance, assessment completion, and examination eligibility are related but separate records.


## 3. Medical Absence and Supporting Evidence

### Rule 11
A student who misses scheduled academic activity because of illness may request consideration under the medical absence procedure. The request should be made through the department's designated process and should identify the dates affected. Medical documentation should state the relevant period of incapacity or treatment. A document that merely says that the student visited a clinic, without identifying the period for which academic activity was affected, may be insufficient for a decision.


### Rule 12
Medical evidence is reviewed by the authority designated by the department. The review is not a guarantee that attendance will be restored or that an examination will be postponed. The authority considers whether the evidence relates to the dates claimed, whether the document is verifiable, and whether the requested academic adjustment is one that the regulations permit.


### Rule 13
For ordinary attendance calculations, an approved medical absence is recorded according to the medical-absence decision. The student should not assume that every medically documented absence is automatically treated as attended. The decision should state the academic effect where an effect is granted. If the decision does not mention an attendance adjustment, the student should ask the department for clarification before relying on it.


### Rule 14
Medical circumstances that arise during an examination require a separate report to the examination office or invigilator as soon as reasonably possible. Leaving an examination without following the reporting procedure can make it difficult to establish the reason later. Students should preserve hospital or clinic records, the examination details, and any communication made on the day.


### Rule 15
The medical procedure is designed for genuine health-related circumstances. It does not define rules for family events, travel problems, employment duties, or ordinary scheduling conflicts. Those circumstances must be considered under any separate rule that specifically covers them. Where the handbook is silent, the student should not infer that the medical exception applies by analogy.


## 4. Attendance Exceptions and Committee Review

### Rule 16
Some cases require a formal exception rather than ordinary attendance processing. The Academic Exceptions Committee may review a documented request where the regulations give it authority to consider an exception. A committee decision must identify the student, the relevant course or period, and the scope of the exception. A general statement that a committee “can help” is not itself an approval.


### Rule 17
A committee may impose conditions when an exception is granted. Conditions can include completion of missed laboratory work, an additional assessment, submission of evidence by a stated date, or another academic requirement that is permitted by the applicable regulation. The conditions should be communicated to the student in writing.


### Rule 18
The committee's power is limited by the authority granted to it. The committee does not automatically acquire every power held by the Examination Board, Finance Office, or Registrar. If a request involves a matter reserved to another authority, the committee may refer the matter instead of deciding it. Students should read the wording of the decision rather than assuming that a referral is an approval.


### Rule 19
The committee may consider exceptional circumstances involving attendance when the relevant rule permits such consideration. A committee waiver should be linked to a particular decision. It should not be treated as a standing change to the university-wide attendance requirement for future semesters.


### Rule 20
Where two rules appear to give different attendance outcomes, the student should not select the more favourable sentence without checking the exception and approval provisions. The handbook expects decision-makers to read related provisions together. If the provisions remain incompatible, the matter should be escalated to the authority responsible for interpreting the regulation.


## 5. Examinations

### Rule 21
End-term examinations are conducted according to a published timetable. Students should verify the date, time, venue, and permitted materials before the examination period. A student who arrives late may be admitted subject to the examination office's instructions. The rules on late arrival are procedural and do not create a general right to extra writing time.


### Rule 22
Students must carry the identification or examination document specified in the examination notice. Invigilators may verify identity before entry. Electronic devices must be handled according to the instructions for that examination. Where calculators or other aids are permitted, the examination notice should state the permitted category.


### Rule 23
A student who becomes unwell during an examination should inform the invigilator immediately. The invigilator records the event and directs the student to the procedure used for examination incidents. A medical certificate obtained later may support a request, but it does not by itself rewrite the examination record.


### Rule 24
Absence from an examination is handled under the examination regulations and any specific absence procedure. The reason for absence matters only if it falls within a recognised ground and the student follows the required reporting process. The handbook does not create a general right to reschedule an examination for every personal circumstance.


### Rule 25
Results are published after the examination and marking process is complete. Students may use the prescribed review or revaluation procedure where available. A request for review should identify the course, examination, and ground on which the request is made. Informal disagreement with a mark is not itself a guarantee of a revised result.


## 6. Continuous Assessment

### Rule 26
Continuous assessment is intended to measure progress during the teaching term. Each course should publish the assessment components and their relative weights. A component may be an assignment, quiz, laboratory, project, presentation, or other activity. Students should check the course outline because different courses can use different combinations while remaining within the programme's assessment framework.


### Rule 27
An assignment submitted after the stated deadline may receive the treatment described in the course outline. Some courses allow a short grace period, while others require approval for late submission. The existence of a late-submission rule does not mean that a student can ignore the deadline without consequence.


### Rule 28
Academic integrity applies to assessed work. Students must follow the collaboration and citation requirements stated by the course or university. Where a course permits group work, each student's contribution should be identifiable when the assessment rules require individual accountability.


### Rule 29
Laboratory work may include attendance, preparation, observation, reports, and practical demonstrations. A missed laboratory should be handled under the course or department procedure. A student should not assume that a laboratory can always be replaced by a written assignment.


### Rule 30
Marks should be recorded against the assessment component to which they belong. Students who identify a calculation or entry error should raise it with the responsible instructor or office within the published review period. The review process should distinguish a clerical correction from a request to change academic judgement.


## 7. Academic Integrity

### Rule 31
Academic integrity means presenting work honestly and respecting the rules of assessment. Plagiarism, fabrication, impersonation, unauthorised collaboration, and prohibited assistance may be treated as academic misconduct. The applicable procedure should identify the allegation, the opportunity to respond, and the authority responsible for the decision.


### Rule 32
Students should keep working notes and source material when an assignment requires independent work. Where collaboration is permitted, the course instructions define its boundaries. A student should not rely on another student's statement that a particular form of assistance is allowed.


### Rule 33
Use of generative software or other digital tools is governed by the course or assessment instructions. If a course does not permit external generation or automated assistance, using such a tool may breach the assessment rule. If the course permits a tool, students should follow any disclosure or citation requirements that accompany that permission.


### Rule 34
An academic integrity review is separate from an ordinary marking review. A student who believes a misconduct allegation is incorrect should use the response process stated in the notice. The fact that an assessment received a low mark does not, by itself, establish misconduct.


### Rule 35
Records of academic integrity decisions are maintained by the designated office. Access to those records is limited according to university policy. A student should use official channels when requesting a copy or clarification of a decision.


## 8. Registration and Course Changes

### Rule 36
Course registration takes place during the period announced by the Registrar. A student should check prerequisites, timetable clashes, credit limits, and programme requirements before confirming a selection. Registration status is reflected in the university's student information system after the relevant processing is complete.


### Rule 37
A course may be added or dropped during the published adjustment period. After the adjustment period, a change may require approval and may have academic or financial consequences. Students should not assume that attending a course creates registration automatically.


### Rule 38
Prerequisites are conditions that normally must be satisfied before a student can register for a course. A prerequisite waiver, where allowed, is a formal approval. It should be recorded against the relevant course and academic session.


### Rule 39
A student taking an approved overload must comply with the approval conditions. An overload approval does not automatically remove timetable, attendance, assessment, or examination requirements.


### Rule 40
Students returning from leave should confirm their registration status before attending classes. The department may need to place the student into a particular curriculum version where the programme structure has changed. The applicable curriculum should be confirmed in writing when the change affects graduation requirements.


## 9. Fees and Payments

### Rule 41
Tuition and other university charges are payable according to the schedule published by the Finance Office. The amount can depend on programme, credit load, accommodation, or other approved categories. Students should use the current fee notice rather than an old screenshot or a fee amount remembered from a previous term.


### Rule 42
The fee schedule in the corpus includes a table of standard payment deadlines. **Rule 9.2:** The standard tuition payment deadline is 15 September. A deadline in the table is the date by which payment should normally be received through the stated channel. Bank processing time may affect when a payment is recorded, so students should not wait until the final hour if the payment method requires processing.


### Rule 43
A late payment can result in a late fee, registration hold, or another consequence if the finance regulations provide for it. The finance office may publish a separate notice for exceptional arrangements. Such an arrangement must be confirmed through an official channel.


### Rule 44
Refunds are processed under the refund policy. The fact that a student stops attending does not automatically establish entitlement to a refund. The refund amount and timing can depend on the date of withdrawal and the category of charge.


### Rule 45
Students who have a fee dispute should first check the transaction record and the invoice. If the record remains inconsistent, the student can contact the Finance Office with the relevant transaction reference. Academic staff should not be treated as the final authority for a finance ledger entry.


## 10. Scholarships and Financial Support

### Rule 46
Scholarships are awarded under the conditions stated in the relevant scholarship notice. Eligibility can involve academic performance, financial need, programme status, or other criteria. A student should read the specific award notice because not every scholarship uses the same conditions.


### Rule 47
A scholarship may be suspended or reviewed if the student no longer meets an eligibility condition. The responsible office should communicate the reason and any opportunity to submit information where the scholarship rules provide one. A temporary academic difficulty does not automatically cancel an award unless the applicable rule says so.


### Rule 48
Students receiving external funding should check whether the funding body imposes conditions in addition to university requirements. The university can confirm its own academic records, but it may not be able to interpret an external sponsor's private rules.


### Rule 49
Financial support applications should be submitted through the announced process and before the stated closing date. Incomplete applications may not be considered if the rules require all supporting documents by the deadline.


### Rule 50
The scholarship office maintains records of applications and awards. Students should keep copies of forms and evidence submitted. Questions about payment timing should be directed to the office responsible for disbursement rather than inferred from the academic calendar.


## 11. Hostel and Campus Conduct

### Rule 51
Students living in university accommodation must follow the residence handbook in addition to general university rules. Room allocation, guest access, quiet hours, maintenance reporting, and common-area use are governed by residence procedures. A residence rule does not automatically change an academic rule.


### Rule 52
Residents should report maintenance issues through the designated system. Emergency hazards should be reported immediately to the warden or security service. Students should not attempt repairs involving electrical, structural, or fire-safety equipment without authorization.


### Rule 53
Guests may be admitted only under the conditions published by the residence office. A resident is responsible for complying with guest-registration procedures. The handbook does not create a general right to overnight guests when a residence rule limits them.


### Rule 54
Quiet hours are intended to protect study and rest. Students should avoid unnecessary noise during the stated period and follow instructions from residence staff. Repeated violations may be handled under the residence disciplinary procedure.


### Rule 55
Hostel charges, deposits, and refund conditions are separate from tuition. A student moving out should follow the checkout process, return university property, and obtain the required clearance. Financial settlement is completed according to the residence office's records.


## 12. Student Services and Complaints

### Rule 56
Students can use academic advising, counselling, library, disability support, career services, and other support services according to their respective procedures. A support service can provide assistance without becoming the decision-maker for an academic or financial rule. Students should therefore distinguish advice from formal approval.


### Rule 57
A complaint should be raised through the channel that handles the subject matter. Academic complaints may go to the department or academic office; finance complaints go to the Finance Office; residence complaints go to the residence office. If a student sends a complaint to the wrong office, the receiving office may redirect it.


### Rule 58
A complaint should state the relevant facts, date, course or service, and the outcome the student is seeking. Supporting records can include notices, receipts, attendance records, or approval letters. A complaint that depends on a document should identify that document so that the reviewer can locate it.


### Rule 59
The university aims to acknowledge formal complaints within its service standards, but acknowledgement is not the same as a decision. Some matters require consultation with another authority. Students should avoid treating silence as approval unless a regulation expressly provides that effect.


### Rule 60
When the handbook does not address a student's exact situation, the safest answer is to say that the corpus does not establish the outcome. The student can then identify the office that appears responsible and ask for a formal interpretation or decision. This principle prevents a general rule from being stretched beyond its wording.


## 13. Library and Learning Resources

### Rule 61
The university library supports teaching and research through print collections, electronic resources, study spaces, and borrowing services. Students must use their own identification when borrowing material and should return items by the due date shown in the library system. Renewal may be available when another user has not reserved the item. A renewal is not valid merely because a student keeps the book beyond the original date without completing the renewal action.


### Rule 62
Library fines and replacement charges are handled under the library's current schedule. A student who believes a charge is incorrect should provide the item record or payment reference. Academic staff cannot normally erase a library transaction simply because the student needs a result or transcript urgently.


### Rule 63
Electronic resources may be subject to licence conditions. Students should use university credentials and should not distribute restricted files or account credentials. A resource being accessible through the campus network does not mean that its licence permits public redistribution.


### Rule 64
Reference and research assistance is available for finding sources, using databases, and understanding citation tools. Library staff can help locate material but do not certify that an academic argument is correct. Students remain responsible for the content of submitted work.


### Rule 65
Study rooms and quiet areas have their own booking or conduct rules. A reservation does not give a student the right to prevent other users from accessing common spaces. Students should leave rooms in the condition in which they found them and report damage through the library process.


## 14. Laboratory Safety

### Rule 66
Laboratory activities must be conducted according to the safety instructions issued for the facility. Students should wear the protective equipment specified for the experiment and should not operate equipment for which they have not received the required orientation. Safety instructions take priority over convenience or speed.


### Rule 67
An incident involving injury, chemical exposure, equipment damage, or another safety concern should be reported immediately to the laboratory supervisor. The incident record is used for safety review and does not by itself determine academic fault.


### Rule 68
Students should use chemicals, instruments, and electrical equipment only for the purpose authorized by the laboratory procedure. Unlabelled containers should not be used. Waste should be placed in the disposal stream specified by the laboratory rather than in an ordinary bin.


### Rule 69
Laboratory attendance is part of the course requirements when the course outline says so. A student who misses a laboratory should follow the course procedure for a missed practical. The safety rules do not create a general substitute activity for an academic absence.


### Rule 70
At the end of a laboratory session, students should shut down equipment as instructed, clean the assigned workspace, and return shared instruments. Damage caused by misuse may be investigated under the applicable university procedure.


## 15. Projects and Dissertations

### Rule 71
Final-year projects and dissertations are supervised academic work. The project registration should identify the student, supervisor, title or area, and any required approval. A change of supervisor or project title should follow the process announced by the department.


### Rule 72
Students are expected to keep a record of project progress and meet the milestones set by the department. A supervisor can provide academic guidance, but a supervisor's informal comment should not be treated as an extension of a formal submission deadline unless the department has approved the change.


### Rule 73
Project submissions must follow the format and submission channel stated by the department. A file sent to a personal email address may not count as an official submission if the department requires the learning-management system or repository.


### Rule 74
Where a project involves human participants, confidential data, or restricted equipment, students must obtain any required ethics, safety, or data approvals before beginning the relevant work. Approval for a project title does not automatically approve every research activity.


### Rule 75
Project evaluation normally considers the components published in the project scheme. Students should keep evidence of their work, version history, and presentation materials where the scheme requires them. Disputes about project marks should use the academic review process. **Rule 15.3 (evaluation clause):** A final project submitted after the published deadline is not accepted unless an extension was approved before the deadline.





## 16. Internships and Field Training

### Rule 76
An internship or field-training activity must be approved when the programme requires approval before the placement begins. Students should confirm the host organization, dates, supervisor, and expected academic deliverables through the designated process.


### Rule 77
Attendance at an internship is normally recorded by the host and may also be monitored by the department. A student who leaves the placement early should inform the department rather than assuming that the host's verbal agreement is enough.


### Rule 78
Internship reports and presentations are assessed according to the programme's published scheme. The host organization's confidential information should not be included in a report unless disclosure is authorized. Students should use anonymised descriptions where the academic requirement permits.


### Rule 79
Travel and accommodation during an internship are the student's responsibility unless a written university arrangement says otherwise. The handbook does not create a general reimbursement entitlement for every placement.


### Rule 80
If an internship is interrupted by illness or another recognised circumstance, the student should contact the department and host promptly. Any extension or replacement requirement depends on the applicable programme procedure.


## 17. Leave of Absence

### Rule 81
Academic leave is a formal change in a student's registration status. A student should apply before the period begins when the regulations require advance approval. Leave granted after a student stops attending may be subject to different conditions.


### Rule 82
During approved leave, a student should not assume that ordinary registration, assessment, or campus-access rights continue unchanged. The approval notice should state the effective dates and any conditions that apply.


### Rule 83
Returning from leave may require confirmation of programme availability, fees, curriculum version, or other administrative details. Students should complete the return process before relying on the timetable for the new term.


### Rule 84
Medical leave and academic leave are related but distinct processes. Medical evidence can support an academic leave request where the regulation allows it, but the medical document itself does not create leave without the required approval.


### Rule 85
A leave approval should be retained with the student's records. If the approval conflicts with a later administrative record, the student should contact the office that issued the approval and provide a copy.


## 18. Accessibility and Support

### Rule 86
Students who need accessibility support should contact the designated support office as early as possible. The office may coordinate reasonable academic arrangements with departments while respecting the student's privacy.


### Rule 87
An approved accommodation applies within the scope stated in the approval. A student should not assume that an arrangement for one course automatically changes the assessment conditions of another course.


### Rule 88
Accessible examination arrangements may include an approved room, additional time, assistive technology, or another adjustment where the relevant authority has approved it. The exact arrangement should be recorded before the examination where practicable.


### Rule 89
Accessibility support is separate from a general attendance waiver. A student who needs an attendance-related adjustment should check whether the approval explicitly covers attendance or only another academic arrangement.


### Rule 90
Students can request a review if their needs change or if an approved arrangement cannot be provided as recorded. The review process should identify the existing approval and the specific difficulty encountered.


## 19. Graduation and Degree Audit

### Rule 91
A student is eligible for graduation only after the programme requirements are satisfied and the university confirms completion. The degree audit considers credits, required courses, minimum performance requirements, and any programme-specific conditions.


### Rule 92
Students should review their degree audit before the final term. A course that appears in a transcript is not necessarily sufficient if it is not the required course or if a prerequisite structure was not satisfied.


### Rule 93
Graduation applications have an administrative deadline. Missing the application deadline can affect the intended ceremony or processing cycle even when academic requirements are already complete.


### Rule 94
Clearance may be required from academic departments, the library, finance, residence, or other offices. Clearance is an administrative confirmation and does not itself create academic credits.


### Rule 95
Where a curriculum has changed, the applicable curriculum version is determined according to the student's admission and programme rules. Students should seek written confirmation if a change affects a graduation requirement.


## 20. Appeals and Review

### Rule 96
An appeal is a formal request to review a decision under the authority provided by the applicable regulation. The appeal should identify the decision, date, grounds, and evidence where the procedure requires those details.


### Rule 97
Appeals are not a general opportunity to replace academic judgement with a preferred outcome. The grounds must match the appeal procedure. A student should read the decision notice to determine the available review route.


### Rule 98
An appeal deadline is normally calculated from the date stated in the relevant notice or decision. Students should not rely on an informal conversation as changing the deadline unless the responsible authority confirms the extension.


### Rule 99
The appeal authority may uphold, vary, set aside, or remit a decision where the regulation gives it that power. The result should be communicated through the official process.


### Rule 100
If a student raises a question that the handbook does not cover, the appeal procedure cannot be assumed to supply the missing substantive rule. The system should distinguish a right to ask for review from a rule that determines the underlying entitlement.



## 21. Digital Records and Official Communication

### Rule 101
The university's student information system is the primary record for registration, course selection, and many academic transactions. Students should check their record after completing an important transaction. A confirmation screen or receipt should be retained when the transaction creates a deadline-sensitive obligation. If the system displays information that differs from an official approval, the student should contact the office responsible for the transaction and provide the approval as evidence.

### Rule 102
Official academic communication may be sent through the university email account, student information system, learning-management system, or another channel named in the relevant procedure. Students are expected to check the channels that apply to their programme. A message sent to a personal account is not necessarily an official notice unless the university has expressly adopted that channel for the purpose.

### Rule 103
A student who changes an email address or telephone number should update the contact information through the university's designated process. Failure to update contact information can make it difficult to receive notices. Updating a contact detail does not change an academic deadline unless the responsible office separately approves an extension.

### Rule 104
Digital copies of approvals, receipts, attendance corrections, and academic decisions should be kept in a form that can be retrieved later. A screenshot can help establish what a student saw at a particular time, but the university may rely on the underlying system record when resolving a dispute. Students should therefore keep both the screenshot and any reference number where available.

### Rule 105
The university may restrict access to records containing personal or academic information. Students must not share another student's academic record, examination result, disciplinary information, or contact details without an appropriate basis. Public availability of a notice does not make every associated student record public.

### Rule 106
When submitting an electronic form, the student should verify that the upload completed and that the confirmation message was generated if the process provides one. An unfinished browser upload is not necessarily a completed submission. If a deadline is close, the student should use the confirmation record or contact the responsible office promptly rather than assuming that an incomplete upload was received.

### Rule 107
Where a department uses a shared repository for project work, students should follow the repository instructions for naming, versioning, and submission. A file being visible in a personal workspace does not necessarily mean that it has been submitted for assessment. The submission instructions for the relevant course or project remain the controlling procedure.

### Rule 108
The university may issue a correction when an official notice contains a clerical error. A correction should identify the notice being corrected and, where relevant, the corrected date or requirement. Students should use the latest official version when the correction clearly replaces the earlier notice.

### Rule 109
Students should not rely on forwarded messages when an official source is available. A forwarded message can lose attachments, context, or later corrections. When a decision affects eligibility, payment, examination, or registration, the student should check the original official notice.

### Rule 110
The university may retain academic records for the period required by its record-management rules. Students requesting access to their own records should use the prescribed request channel. A request for access does not itself change the content of the record or extend any academic deadline.


## 22. Student Wellbeing and Campus Safety

### Rule 111
Students are encouraged to seek support when academic pressure, personal circumstances, or another difficulty affects their ability to study. Counselling and wellbeing services can explain available support options and can help students identify the correct administrative route. Support staff do not automatically grant academic extensions unless the applicable authority gives them that power.

### Rule 112
A student who believes there is an immediate threat to personal safety should contact campus security or the emergency service identified by the university. Academic procedures should not be used as a substitute for an emergency response. After an emergency, the student may use the relevant academic or support procedure to document any resulting academic difficulty.

### Rule 113
Students must follow fire-safety instructions in classrooms, laboratories, residences, and other university buildings. Emergency exits must remain clear. A student should not disable an alarm or obstruct emergency equipment. Safety staff may direct people to leave a building even when a scheduled academic activity is underway.

### Rule 114
Health and safety instructions for laboratories, workshops, studios, and field activities may impose requirements that are more specific than general campus rules. Students should follow the instruction applicable to the activity. The existence of a general campus rule does not remove a programme's responsibility to communicate activity-specific safety requirements.

### Rule 115
A student who has a temporary health condition that affects participation in an academic activity should contact the relevant support or academic office to discuss the available process. The student should not assume that a health condition automatically changes an assessment requirement. Any academic adjustment should be recorded through the applicable procedure.

### Rule 116
Wellbeing appointments are normally confidential within the limits of the service's professional and legal obligations. Academic departments should receive only the information needed to implement an approved academic arrangement. Students who want an arrangement should ask the support office what documentation is necessary.

### Rule 117
The university may run workshops on study skills, time management, examination preparation, and student wellbeing. Attendance at a voluntary workshop does not count as course attendance unless a course outline expressly includes the activity as an assessed or compulsory component.

### Rule 118
A student who is concerned about another student's immediate safety should use the university's safety or support channels rather than attempting to manage a serious emergency alone. The reporting process should provide enough information for the responsible service to respond while avoiding unnecessary disclosure of private information.

### Rule 119
Campus safety reports and academic complaints are separate processes. A safety report can trigger an operational response, but it does not automatically establish an academic entitlement. Where an incident affects an examination, attendance, or assessment, the student should also use the academic process that applies to that consequence.

### Rule 120
The university may review safety procedures after an incident and issue updated instructions. Students should follow the current instruction for the location and activity. An old safety poster or forwarded message should not be treated as current when an official update has replaced it.
